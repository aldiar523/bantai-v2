How BantAI works

BantAI reads the full list of DPWH public works contracts every week and marks open contracts worth a closer look. It does not estimate the probability that a contract is corrupt. A mark is a request to check, never an accusation.

Goal, and why we changed course

The first version ranked contracts by red flags weighted by how well they predicted contract terminations and citizen complaints. Two problems followed. A termination is not corruption. And when the flags were tested against documented problem contracts (COA audit findings, the ghost-project list, Sandiganbayan charge sheets), most did not separate them from ordinary contracts.

Only about 260 problem contracts can be tied to a DPWH contract ID in open sources. That is too few to prove that any single flag predicts wrongdoing. So version 2 does not ask a flag to prove itself on past cases. Each check must instead rest on the law, an official record, or the mechanism of a known scheme, and the list relies on several independent signs pointing the same way.

The aim is practical: give auditors, journalists and civil society a short, explained list of open contracts, while the money is still being spent.

Weekly process

  1. Read. Every Monday the full DPWH project list is saved (about 267,000 contracts), with the changes since last week.
  2. Join. Each contract is joined, by its contract ID, to PhilGEPS notices and awards, bid records, government performance evaluations (CPES), PCAB licences and sanctions, and the register of documented problem contracts.
  3. Check. Every open contract (for procurement, not yet started, on-going) goes through the official-record checks, the rule checks and the seven signal families below.
  4. Rank. Mark A first, then B, then C (three or more families). Inside each mark, the largest contracts first. No office may hold more than 30% of the top 50.
  5. Seal. The list is saved and its SHA-256 fingerprint is written to a public, append-only ledger. Later charges, audits and statements can be compared with what was listed before they became public.
  6. Review. Reviewers read the documents, may ask for an AI review, and record a decision: escalate, monitor, no concern, or data error. A person decides.

Each contract page lists every check as triggered, clear, or not checked because data are missing. A family counts once however many of its flags fire; flags built from the same fields sit in the same family, so one fact is never counted twice.

In short: open data alone cannot yet prove which signals predict corruption, because too few problem contracts carry an ID. BantAI therefore relies on law, official records and mechanism, labels each mark by its basis, and seals each week's list so it can be tested forward.

AI review

A reviewer can ask for an AI review of a contract. It summarises the file, looks for ordinary explanations (a calamity, a design change, a joint venture, a project finished but not updated), searches news, official statements and court records, and suggests the next document and site checks. Every outside claim links to a source it opened. It is advice: it never changes a mark or a decision, never calls anyone corrupt, and is seen by reviewers only. Satellite images of the site over time (Esri World Imagery archive) are on each contract page for the reviewer's own eyes; automated change detection did not work in our tests.

Publication and data protection

  • A mark is a request to look. Status words come from the source ("charged", "revoked"), never stronger.
  • Private individuals are not named: no company officers, owners or employees, even where public registers list them.
  • Marks B and C, AI reviews and decisions are encrypted on this site and open only with the review team's key, in the reviewer's own browser. The key is never sent anywhere.
  • The code and working data stay in a private repository; this site holds only built pages, public data and the encrypted reviewer file.
  • Open, legal data only. No requests for non-public records.

Limits

  • DPWH statuses lag: about 7,300 contracts report 100% done but are still listed as on-going. Since v2.1 they count as "reported complete" and leave the open-contract queue; a contract re-enters if its progress drops below 100%.
  • Coordinates are as DPWH gives them. For buildings they mark the building site; where many contracts share a point it may be an office or a placeholder.
  • Contracts still in procurement rarely reach the list: the winner and bids are not known yet.
  • Tender and price checks cover only contracts with bid records or stated road lengths; a missing check lowers a score, it never raises it.
  • No signal family is yet shown to beat random on known problem contracts. The weekly sealed lists exist so that this can be tested as new cases become public.
